Ko-fi markets itself as the "zero-fee" platform for creators. It's a compelling pitch — especially when Patreon is taking 10% off the top. But the pitch comes with qualifiers. Ko-fi's free tier genuinely does charge 0% on one-off tips, and that part is the honest core of it. Monthly tips, memberships, commissions, and shop sales are 5%. And zero across the board means Ko-fi Gold, which costs $12 per month. Then there's the payment processing fee that every platform charges — roughly 2.9% plus $0.30 per transaction — which none of them are eager to highlight. The real question isn't which platform has the lowest advertised fee. It's which one leaves the most money in your pocket after every deduction is applied to your specific revenue mix. You can [model your Ko-fi numbers directly](/creator/kofi-calculator) to see exact take-home, but this guide walks through the full comparison first.

## How Each Platform Charges You

All three platforms — Ko-fi, Patreon, and Buy Me a Coffee — use the same basic model: a percentage-based platform fee on your earnings, plus payment processing fees passed through from Stripe or PayPal. The differences come down to the platform fee rate, what triggers it, and what you get in return.

### Ko-fi: Three Fee States

Ko-fi has three fee states, not two. On **Ko-fi Free**, one-off tips (called "coffees") and crowdfunding goals carry a 0% service fee, while monthly tips, memberships, commissions, and shop sales carry 5%. **Standard** is what Ko-fi now switches on for new creators by default — it unlocks the full feature set and charges 5% on every payment type, including one-off tips. It's reversible from Settings → Payment at any time. **Ko-fi Gold** costs $12 per month and charges 0% on everything. One catch worth knowing: a Gold creator who also has the Standard setting switched on still pays the 5%, and has to opt out of it separately.

### Patreon: One Rate

Patreon charges 10% on the standard plan, applied to all patron payments and one-time purchases. There's no plan to choose — the old menu closed on 4 August 2025, and every creator who published a page after that date is on 10%. A few legacy plans survive for the creators already on them (Founders at 5%, Pro at 8%, Pro + Merch at 11%), but they're closed to everyone else, and unpublishing your page forfeits them permanently. For a deeper breakdown, see our [full guide to Patreon fees](/blog/patreon-fees-and-earnings).

### Buy Me a Coffee: Flat Rate

Buy Me a Coffee charges a flat 5% on all transactions — one-time coffees and memberships alike. No tier selection, no upsells, and no paid upgrade that reduces it. Processing sits on top at 2.9% plus $0.30, with a 0.5% surcharge on recurring payments and another 1% on transactions outside the US.

One number we can't pin down: Buy Me a Coffee also lists a 0.5% Stripe payout processing charge, and its own help pages are ambiguous about whether that's billed to you on top or recovered through the surcharges above. Read every Buy Me a Coffee figure below as a floor, not a final answer.

### Payment Processing: Universal

Regardless of platform, every transaction incurs a payment processing fee of roughly 2.9% plus $0.30. This is the Stripe or PayPal cut, and no platform absorbs it for you. It's always on top of the platform fee.

One structural difference is worth flagging early: Ko-fi never holds your money. You connect your own PayPal or Stripe account and pay whatever rate that account is on. Ko-fi's own guidance is "usually around 3% + $0.30", and it says plainly that the real figure varies by account type, location, and currency. Patreon and Buy Me a Coffee process on your behalf at their own published rates.

## Ko-fi: The Zero-Fee Promise

Ko-fi's positioning as a creator-friendly alternative to Patreon hinges on two things: the option to accept tips with no recurring commitment from supporters, and the Gold tier's 0% platform fee.

On the free tier, Ko-fi takes 0% of one-off tips and 5% of monthly tips, memberships, commissions, and shop sales. For a creator whose income is mostly spontaneous one-off support, that 0% isn't marketing — it's the actual rate, and nothing else in this comparison matches it. What the free tier costs you is features: no supporter-only content, no scheduled posts, no shortened page name, less shop storage.

Ko-fi Gold, at $12 per month, drops the service fee to 0% across every payment type. The key distinction: Gold is a flat monthly cost, not a percentage. That means it becomes more valuable as your revenue increases — and there's a specific crossover point where it starts saving you money.

One caveat before you plan around Gold. Ko-fi is visibly de-emphasising it: the Gold page itself now leads by telling creators they no longer need Gold, and the Help Center's own fee breakdown table lists only Free and Standard. Gold is still listed at $12 on the pricing page and Ko-fi's FAQ still says you can join, but confirm it's on offer before building a budget on it.

## Ko-fi Gold Break-Even Math

The math here is straightforward, but the base matters more than the arithmetic. Gold's 0% is only worth something against revenue that would otherwise carry the 5% service fee — monthly tips, memberships, commissions, and shop sales. One-off tips on Ko-fi Free are already 0%, so a pure tip-jar creator saves nothing at all by upgrading, however much they earn.

For fee-bearing revenue, the break-even is where 5% equals $12 per month. That happens at $240 per month.

If you're earning $240 per month in fee-bearing revenue, the 5% costs you exactly $12 — the same as Gold. Below $240, the percentage model is cheaper: 5% of $100 is only $5, against Gold's flat $12. Above $240, Gold saves you money on every additional dollar.

At $500 per month in fee-bearing revenue, the 5% costs $25 in service fees. Gold costs $12. You save $13 per month, or $156 per year.

At $1,000 per month, the 5% costs $50. Gold saves you $38 per month, or $456 per year.

At $2,000 per month, the 5% costs $100. Gold saves you $88 per month, or $1,056 per year.

The economics are clear enough, but the threshold is twice what most comparison articles still quote — including an earlier version of this one — because Gold used to cost $6 and the break-even used to be $120. It isn't any more. If you're consistently earning more than $240 per month in fee-bearing revenue on Ko-fi, Gold pays for itself. Below that, it's a subscription that costs more than the fee it removes.

## Patreon: The Membership Standard

Patreon built the category of creator memberships, and it still has the strongest brand recognition for recurring support. When a viewer or listener hears "support me on Patreon," they know exactly what that means. That recognition has real value — it reduces friction when you're converting casual fans into paying supporters.

### Where Patreon Excels

Patreon's standard plan gives you robust membership tiers, analytics dashboards, integration with Discord and other platforms, and a more polished patron experience. The content-gating tools are more mature than Ko-fi's, and the patron app makes it easy for supporters to manage their pledges. For creators building a serious membership business — especially those with multiple content tiers and a large patron base — Patreon's tooling justifies the higher fee.

### Where the Fee Structure Hurts

The standard 10% applies to everyone who joined after August 2025, and it's double Buy Me a Coffee's 5% and a long way from Ko-fi Gold's 0%. On $500 in monthly patron revenue, you're paying $50 in platform fees on Patreon versus $12 for a month of Ko-fi Gold. That's a $38 monthly difference — $456 per year — going to the platform instead of your pocket.

The second structural issue only shows up at small pledge sizes. Patreon's standard plan has no micropayment processing rate: it charges 2.9% plus $0.30 on every payment regardless of amount, where the closed legacy plans charged 5% plus $0.10 on tiers priced at $3 or less. On a $3 pledge that's $0.39 in processing instead of $0.25, per patron, every month — and there's no plan you can switch to that brings it back.

The third is one-off support. It isn't shop sales, despite what a lot of comparisons claim — Ko-fi charges 5% on shop sales too, on both Free and Standard. It's tips. Patreon has no real one-off tipping product, and Ko-fi's free tier takes 0% of one. If a meaningful share of your income is spontaneous rather than recurring, that gap is the whole argument.

## Buy Me a Coffee: The Simple Middle Ground

Buy Me a Coffee occupies the space between Ko-fi and Patreon with a straightforward value proposition: 5% on everything, no tier selection required, no upsells.

### What You Get

The platform supports one-time donations ("coffees"), recurring memberships, and a simple shop for digital products. The interface is deliberately minimal — set up takes minutes, and the supporter experience is clean and friction-free. For creators who want a "support me" button without managing a full membership platform, Buy Me a Coffee delivers.

### What You Don't Get

The simplicity comes with limitations. Buy Me a Coffee lacks the deep analytics Patreon offers, the shop flexibility Ko-fi provides, and the commission system Ko-fi has built out. Membership tier options are more basic than either competitor. There's no equivalent of Ko-fi Gold to reduce your fees as you scale.

### The Fee Reality

At 5%, Buy Me a Coffee matches what Ko-fi charges on memberships and shop sales, and undercuts Patreon's standard plan by five percentage points. But unlike Ko-fi, there's no way to reduce that 5% as your revenue grows. A creator taking $1,000 a month in recurring support pays $50 in platform fees plus the 0.5% recurring surcharge — $55 — every month, with no option to lower it. On Ko-fi Gold, the same creator pays $12: a difference of $43 per month.

Where Buy Me a Coffee loses outright is one-off tips. Ko-fi's free tier charges 0% on those; Buy Me a Coffee charges its 5% on everything. For creators earning under $240 per month in fee-bearing revenue, Buy Me a Coffee and Ko-fi's 5% states are close to identical. Above that threshold, Ko-fi Gold pulls ahead. Buy Me a Coffee's advantage is simplicity, not pricing.

## Payment Processing: The Fee That Hits Everyone

Regardless of which platform you choose, payment processing fees take a cut of every transaction. The standard rate across Ko-fi, Patreon, and Buy Me a Coffee is approximately 2.9% plus $0.30 per transaction, passed through from Stripe or PayPal.

The percentage component scales predictably. On a $5 donation, 2.9% is $0.15. On a $50 shop order, it's $1.45. That part is proportional and manageable.

The $0.30 flat fee per transaction is where the economics get painful on small amounts. On a $3 coffee (Ko-fi's default donation amount), $0.30 represents 10% of the transaction — just from the flat fee. Add the 2.9% percentage component ($0.09), and payment processing alone costs $0.39 on a $3 donation. That's a 12.9% payment processing fee before the platform even takes its cut. On Ko-fi's free tier, for a one-off tip, that processing fee is the entire cut.

On a $5 donation, payment processing is $0.45 (8.9%). On a $10 donation, it's $0.59 (5.9%). On a $25 membership, it's $1.03 (4.1%).

This math is close to identical across all three platforms, with the caveats above: Buy Me a Coffee's recurring and international surcharges, Patreon's higher 3.9% rate for PayPal and Venmo payments from outside the US, and the fact that Ko-fi's rate is whatever your own processor charges you rather than a rate Ko-fi negotiated. No platform has negotiated meaningfully better payment processing rates for creators. The only way to reduce the per-transaction impact is to increase your average transaction size — which means encouraging larger donations, higher membership tiers, or bundled shop orders.

This is also why [TikTok's Creator Fund](/blog/tiktok-creator-earnings) and ad-based revenue models feel so different from supporter platforms — there are no per-transaction fees eating into micro-payments because the payment structure is fundamentally different.

## Real Revenue Comparison

Let's put concrete numbers on the same scenario across all three platforms. You have 20 supporters each paying $5 per month as a recurring membership. Gross monthly revenue: $100. Because this is recurring rather than one-off, Ko-fi's 5% applies on the free tier too.

### Ko-fi Free or Standard

Service fee (5%): $5.00. Payment processing (2.9% of $100 + 20 x $0.30): $2.90 + $6.00 = $8.90. Total fees: $13.90. Net: $86.10. Effective fee: 13.9%.

### Ko-fi Gold

Service fee (0%): $0.00. Gold subscription: $12.00. Payment processing: $8.90. Total cost: $20.90. Net: $79.10. Effective cost: 20.9%.

At $100 per month, Gold is the most expensive option on this page. You're well below the $240 break-even point. Gold doesn't make sense until your fee-bearing revenue consistently exceeds $240.

### Patreon

Platform fee (10%): $10.00. Payment processing (2.9% of $100 + 20 x $0.30): $8.90. Total fees: $18.90. Net: $81.10. Effective fee: 18.9%.

### Buy Me a Coffee

Platform fee (5%): $5.00. Recurring payment surcharge (0.5%): $0.50. Payment processing: $8.90. Total fees: $14.40. Net: $85.60. Effective fee: 14.4%.

### The Takeaway

At 20 supporters and $5 each, Ko-fi's 5% states win at $86.10 net, with Buy Me a Coffee $0.50 behind and Patreon $5.00 behind. Ko-fi Gold costs you $7.00 a month more than simply not subscribing to it.

Now scale it to 50 supporters at $5 each ($250 gross):

- Ko-fi Free or Standard: $250 - $12.50 (service) - $22.25 (processing) = $215.25. Effective: 13.9%.
- Ko-fi Gold: $250 - $0 (service) - $12.00 (Gold) - $22.25 (processing) = $215.75. Effective: 13.7%.
- Patreon: $250 - $25.00 (platform) - $22.25 (processing) = $202.75. Effective: 18.9%.
- Buy Me a Coffee: $250 - $12.50 (platform) - $1.25 (recurring surcharge) - $22.25 (processing) = $214.00. Effective: 14.4%.

At $250 a month you've only just crossed Gold's break-even, and it shows: Gold beats the 5% states by $0.50 a month, which is not a reason to subscribe to anything. Against Patreon, though, it's $13 a month — $156 a year. Gold doesn't get genuinely interesting until your fee-bearing revenue is several times the break-even.

## One-Time vs Recurring: Different Platforms for Different Models

The choice between these platforms isn't just about fees — it's about how your audience prefers to support you.

### Ko-fi: Built for Tips and Flexibility

Ko-fi's origin as a "buy me a coffee" button means it excels at one-time support. The friction is extremely low — a supporter can send you $3 to $5 without setting up a subscription or making any ongoing commitment. It's also the one product Ko-fi doesn't charge a service fee on if you stay on the free tier, so the economics and the ergonomics point the same way. For creators whose audiences prefer spontaneous support over monthly pledges, this matters.

Ko-fi also supports memberships, but the membership tools are less mature than Patreon's. You get recurring payments and member-only content, but the patron management, content gating, and community features aren't as deep. If memberships are your primary revenue model and you need robust tiering, analytics, and integrations, Patreon's tooling is stronger.

### Patreon: Built for Subscriptions

Patreon is engineered for recurring revenue. The entire platform — tiers, goals, patron milestones, anniversary rewards — is designed to convert one-time visitors into long-term subscribers and reduce churn. If your content model supports monthly deliverables (a podcast, video series, newsletter, or community), Patreon's infrastructure is purpose-built for that.

The downside is that Patreon is awkward for one-time support. There's no clean way for a fan to send you a one-time $5 without subscribing. Some creators work around this by creating a $1 "tip" tier, but it's clunky compared to Ko-fi's one-click donation flow.

### Buy Me a Coffee: Built for Simplicity

Buy Me a Coffee handles both one-time and recurring, but neither with the depth of the specialist. It's the generalist option — good enough at everything, best-in-class at nothing. For creators who want a single simple link that accepts both tips and memberships without requiring them to learn a complex platform, it fits.

## Which Platform Fits Your Creator Type

The right platform depends on your revenue model, audience behavior, and how much complexity you're willing to manage.

### Choose Ko-fi If...

You earn primarily through one-time tips and occasional support, which Ko-fi's free tier takes 0% of — the single strongest argument on this page. Your audience prefers low-commitment interactions, dropping $5 when they enjoy something rather than subscribing monthly. You sell digital products or commissions alongside tips, accepting the 5% those carry. Or your fee-bearing revenue is comfortably over $240 per month, where Gold's 0% starts paying for itself.

### Choose Patreon If...

Your income model is subscription-based with monthly content deliverables. You need mature membership tiers, analytics, and integrations (Discord, Vimeo, WordPress). Your audience already understands and expects Patreon. You're earning enough, at a high enough average pledge, that the standard 10% is justified by the tooling. You prioritize patron management and churn-reduction features over raw fee savings.

### Choose Buy Me a Coffee If...

You want the simplest possible setup with minimal ongoing management. Your revenue is modest (under $240 per month in fee-bearing revenue) and you don't need Ko-fi Gold's savings. You want both tips and memberships on one platform without choosing between donation-first (Ko-fi) and subscription-first (Patreon) philosophies. You value simplicity over flexibility or feature depth.

### The Multi-Platform Approach

Some creators run Ko-fi for tips and shop sales while maintaining a Patreon for memberships. This captures both revenue models at their optimal fee structures but requires managing two platforms, two audiences, and two sets of content. It works best for creators who are already established on one platform and want to add a complementary income stream without migrating their existing supporters.
